How it goes

  1. Scoping call

    30 minutes, free. We agree which deadlines are in, and I confirm the price and the day you get the forecast.

  2. You connect your tracker

    The same read-only connection as the Scan. A file export works too, if your security team prefers that.

  3. A working session

    45 to 60 minutes with you, and a team lead if useful. What your statuses mean, which deadlines were promised and to whom, and what gets built first.

  4. I build the model

    I map how work really moves through your team and run the simulations for every deadline on your roadmap.

  5. We read it together

    It takes an hour. You get the forecast as a PDF right after.

  6. Four weeks later

    I run the numbers again and send a short note on what moved.

Is this for you?

Yes, when

  • You have several deadlines ahead, and the same team behind all of them.
  • You answer for dates you can no longer check ticket by ticket.
  • Your tracker has at least twelve weeks of history, however messy.

Start somewhere else when

  • You have one deadline. Run the free Scan, then book a call about your result.
  • You work in Notion, Excel or on a whiteboard. Start with the Delivery Setup.
  • The question is what to build, not when it lands.

Your data

Read-only. For the forecast I keep the data until the second run, then delete it.

Method and data →

I use

When work moved between statuses, blocker flags, how items group into epics, created and resolved dates, when items were added, sprint boundaries.

I don't use

Descriptions, comments, attachments or code.

Questions people ask first

What if our Jira is a mess?

That's expected. I don't take the tracker at face value. I build a model of how work actually flows before I run any numbers.

Who sees the forecast?

You do. It goes to nobody else unless you forward it.

What if the numbers are bad?

Then you know while there is still time to change the date, the scope or the pace. The forecast says which one I would pick.
