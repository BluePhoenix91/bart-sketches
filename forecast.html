What you leave with

One page for your CEO or board

The summary is written to be forwarded as it is. "The forecast says 23%" is something you can say in a board meeting. "I have a bad feeling" is not.

The chance on each milestone

A probability, a realistic date and a safe date for up to five milestones, counted in the order you plan to build them.

The reasons

What is stuck, what grew after the date was set, what waits on something else. I read that in your data myself. About the system, never about people.

What to do this week

Which milestone to move, cut or swap, and what to stop starting. When nothing needs doing, the forecast says so.

A second run after four weeks

The same numbers again, with a short note on what moved and what didn't.

An hour with me

We read the forecast together, so you hear what I'd do first and can push back.

Why it isn't the Scan five times

Five Scans can each say yes while the roadmap says no.

A Scan asks one question: if the team did only this, would it make the date? Your team never does only one thing. Every date on the roadmap counts on the same people.

One team, counted once

Your milestones go in the order you plan to build them. The third one starts when the first two are done, and its chance says so.

The reasons, read by a person

The simulation doesn't know that one item waits on another, or that work gets started out of order. I look for that in your data and write it down in plain words.

A decision, not a list of numbers

Which date to move, what to cut and what to build first. On a page your CEO or board can read without you in the room.

The forecast assumes you finish milestones in the order you give me. If the team works on everything at once, I show you what that costs.

How it goes

  1. Scoping call

    30 minutes, free. We agree which milestones are in, and I confirm the price and the day you get the forecast.

  2. You connect your tracker

    The same read-only connection as the Scan. A file export works too, if your security team prefers that.

  3. One working session

    45 to 60 minutes with you, and a team lead if useful. What your statuses mean, which milestones were promised, to whom, and in what order you plan to build them.

  4. I build the model

    I map how work really moves through your team and run 10,000 simulations per milestone, each on top of the ones before it.

  5. We read it together

    One hour. You get the forecast as a PDF right after.

  6. Four weeks later

    I run the numbers again and send a short note on what moved.

Is this for you?

Yes, when

  • You have a roadmap: several packages of work, one after the other or side by side.
  • You answer for dates you can no longer check ticket by ticket.
  • Your tracker has at least twelve weeks of history, however messy.

Start somewhere else when

  • You have one package of work and one date. Run the free Scan, then book a read-out.
  • There is no usable tracker history. Start with the first two days of the Realign.
  • The question is what to build, not when it lands.

Team size doesn't decide this. One team with a six-month roadmap is a Forecast. Five teams with one date each is five Scans.

I never read

Descriptions, comments, attachments or code.

Questions people ask first

What if our Jira is a mess?

That's expected. I don't take the tracker at face value. I build a model of how work actually flows before I run any numbers.

Who sees the forecast?

You do. It goes to nobody else unless you forward it.

What if the numbers are bad?

Then you know while there is still time to change the date, the scope or the pace. The forecast says which one I would pick.

Does the model know about dependencies?

No. It counts your team once and your milestones in order. Whether one item waits on another, I read in your data and report in words. My advice is usually to remove the dependency, not to plan around it.
