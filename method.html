What it captures, and what it can't

It captures

The full range of speeds your team has actually had. The weeks reviews dragged, someone was out, a dependency didn't land. The patterns your team already lives with.

It can't

Predict what has never happened. If your lead architect leaves next Tuesday, no model saw that coming. That's rarely what catches teams, though. It's the slow build-up of ordinary weeks.

The realistic date is the median: half of the 10,000 runs finished earlier, half later. It is not a promise.

Why not a dashboard?

Tools like ActionableAgile, LinearB or Jellyfish are good at what they do: they show an engineering team its own flow. They tell you where you are.

The forecast tells you where you're going to end up, for a date you've already promised to someone.
