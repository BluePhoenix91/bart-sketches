See how work really moves, then rebuild it

Day one

I sit in your daily meeting, open your board, and talk to the two or three people closest to planning and development. I map how work actually flows, not how it's supposed to.

Day two

We rebuild the board together, write the priority list for the next four weeks, and fix the meetings. You get my assessment in writing.

What you have on Monday

Phase 2 · Four to six weeks · Fixed price per team · Fewer days every week

Stay while the new setup meets real life

This is where quick fixes fail. A priority changes, a dependency doesn't land, someone shows up with something urgent. I'm there in those moments, so the team handles them with the new setup instead of the old habits.

  • Coaching in the daily meeting, at the board and in scope conversations
  • Cleaning up the backlog with your PM or tech lead
  • Adjusting the setup while we're still working together

A written handover: what changed, why, how to keep it, and what to watch. A final run of the Odds Report. A check-in four weeks after I leave.

Questions people ask first

Are you in our office every day?

No. The two days of phase 1, yes. In phase 2 I'm there for the moments that matter, fewer every week. Phase 3 is handover.

Can't we just run a retrospective?

You can, and you should. But a retro surfaces what the team already knows. I don't work here, so I see what nobody sees because they're too close.

What if the two days turn out to be enough?

Then that's the whole engagement. The setup is yours, and the four-week check-in tells us whether it held.
