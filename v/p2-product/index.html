An excerpt from a real sample report

This is page 1 of the sample audit, written for a fictional company called AC-ME: a SaaS company with a product team of 8 developers. Yours is about your team, your deadlines and your numbers.

Sample audit · page 1 · for your CEO

AC-ME has promised four dates for the first half of 2027. Checked one at a time, all four look safe. Built in the order the team plans, the first two hold, the third is a coin flip and the fourth is late. The team's pace is steady. There is more work than time, and the last date realistically lands about three weeks late.

Where each deadline stands

Payment infrastructure v2: on track, 98%. Realistic February 9, promised February 15.

Customer portal redesign: on track, 84%, with no margin. Realistic April 1, promised March 31.

Mobile app refresh: at risk, 53%. Realistic May 11, promised April 30.

Reporting dashboard: critical, 23%. Realistic June 23, promised May 31.

What to change first, from the same page

  1. Decide which date can move: the mobile app refresh or the reporting dashboard. Both can't hold.
  2. Finish in order. Stop starting work on the mobile refresh and the dashboard until payments has shipped.
  3. Review before starting new work. 7 items are built and waiting for a review today. A waiting review goes before anything new.
  4. Close the scope of the customer portal. It grew by 9 items since December, and every item pushes the two deadlines behind it.

For the reporting dashboard the report also shows three ways to make the date hold: promise June 23, take 30 items out, or build the dashboard before the mobile refresh. Then it says which one I would do first.

What I did in two teams where the dates mattered

Real estate marketplace for CIB Vlaanderen · first product hire

Spotto: the promise I wouldn't make

Two months after launch I was asked to commit to the quarter's plan: 14 items by 30 June, with at most 10% left undone. I said I couldn't promise it. 13 of the 14 were accepted eight days early.

What I did: I became the first stop for every question and bug report, so answers went from days to minutes. I wrote the acceptance tests down and held back a release that wasn't good enough the evening before. I planned one quarter at a time and checked progress every week or two. Work coming back after review or testing fell from 61% to 21%.

A forecast on that team's own record, run three years later, gave the plan an 86% chance of holding. The number I needed was already in our tracking history.

Questions before you book the first call

Is the Audit worth €4,500 if the Scan already gives me a number?

Only when the date can't move and neither can the scope. The Scan gives you the chance. The Audit says why it is that chance and what to change first, in writing, for up to five deadlines.

My deadline is a month away. Is this too late?

No. For that date you get, 14 days after the first call, where it really lands and what each option does to it: a later date, less scope or a different order. You can take those decisions the same day. A month is too short for changing how the team works, and that part of the report is for your next date.

What if the numbers are bad?

Then you know while there is still time to move the date, cut scope or change how the team works. The report says which I would do first.

Can I promise the realistic date?

With its chance next to it. It is the date that 85 of 100 runs made, so 15 finished later. It is also the best case: it assumes the work stays as it is today and your team keeps its recent pace. The second measurement shows how far it moved.

What if our tracker is a mess?

That's expected. I don't take the tracker at face value. In the working session you tell me what your statuses really mean, and I check how work actually moved before I run any numbers.

We have more than five deadlines.

Start with the five that worry you most. What holds those back usually holds the others back too. Want them all in? You get a price on the first call, before you decide.

Do you need our estimates or story points?

No. The forecast uses what your team finished and when, not what anyone estimated.

How do I know the number is right?

It isn't an opinion. It's your team's own history, played forward 10,000 times. I also checked 880 software tasks with the same estimate: half were done within three days and one in twenty took longer than four weeks. There was no better estimate to find. The method and the data behind it are public.

What do you use from our tracker?

When work moved between statuses, blocker flags, how items group into epics, created and resolved dates, when items were added and sprint boundaries. Not descriptions, comments, attachments or code. The connection is read-only and the raw tracker data is never stored.

Who sees the Audit?

You do. It goes to nobody else unless you forward it.
