How the number is made, in one sentence

I take what your team really finished in each of the last twelve weeks, replay the work that is left 10,000 times, and count how many of those runs are done by your date.

That count is your chance. It needs no estimates and no story points. Where the numbers come from is on the method and data page.

What to do with 34%

34% sits on the left of the bar, in the miss likely part. About 1 in 3 of the 10,000 runs made March 15. A coin flip sits in the middle and hit likely sits on the right. The realistic date is the one 85 of 100 runs made: May 24, 2027, which is 10 weeks late.

The card shows three ways to close the gap. Ship later: March 15 becomes May 24. Ship less: 67 items become 42. Ship faster: 50% more speed.

If you can still move the date or cut scope, the card is all you need. If neither can move, something will give anyway. That is when the Audit makes sense: it starts from the same calculation and goes after the why.

Two worries I hear first

My deadline is less than a month away

That is not too late. The Audit takes 14 days after the first call, and for that date it tells you where it really lands and what each option does to it: a later date, less scope or a different order. Those are decisions you can take the same day. A month is too short for changing how the team works, and that part of the report is for your next date. And the Scan gives you a first number today.

Our tracker is a mess

Both the Scan and the Audit need a tracker with at least twelve weeks of history, however messy. The Scan counts an item as done on the day your tracker marks it resolved, so if your releases come later than that, your real date is later too. In the Audit I don't take the tracker at face value: you tell me what your statuses really mean, and I check how work actually moved before I run any numbers.

Questions before you book the first call

What if the numbers are bad?

Then you know while there is still time to move the date, cut scope or change how the team works. The report says which I would do first. If the numbers are good, it says that too.

Can I promise the realistic date?

With its chance next to it. It is the date that 85 of 100 runs made, so 15 finished later. It is also the best case: it assumes the work stays as it is today and your team keeps its recent pace.

Do you need our estimates or story points?

No. The forecast uses what your team finished and when, not what anyone estimated.

How do I know the number is right?

It isn't an opinion. It's your team's own history, played forward 10,000 times. I also checked 880 software tasks with the same estimate: half were done within three days and one in twenty took longer than four weeks. There was no better estimate to find.
