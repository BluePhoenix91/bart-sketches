How the Scan works: three steps to your number

In one sentence: the Scan looks at how much work your team finished in each of the last twelve weeks, replays the weeks ahead 10,000 times in that same pattern, and counts how often the work is done by your date.

1. Connect your tracker read-only

Jira Cloud or Azure DevOps. The Scan reads status changes, created and resolved dates and issue types. It cannot change, create or delete anything.

2. Pick the project and the date

Choose the project that worries you and enter the date you promised, or the one you are about to promise. It takes about three minutes in total.

3. Get the chance and what closes the gap

You see the chance, the realistic date, and how much later or how much less closes the gap. There is no estimate or story point to enter.

Where the number comes from, and what if the tracker is a mess

Where does a number like 34% come from? Take the example above: 67 items, promised for March 15. I do not ask the team for a better estimate. I take what they finished in the last twelve weeks, fast weeks and slow ones, and play the 67 items forward 10,000 times. 3,400 of those runs are done by March 15. That is the 34%.

I checked 880 software tasks with the same kind of work, the same estimate and the same process. Half were done within three days. One in twenty took longer than four weeks. It is public data, so you can check it, and it is why the Scan gives a chance and not a single date. Method and data →

What if our tracker is a mess? The Scan still runs. It counts an item as done on the day your tracker marks it resolved. If your releases come later than that, so does your real date.

The Delivery Audit expects a mess. I do not take the tracker at face value: in the working session you tell me what your statuses really mean, and I check how work actually moved before I run any numbers. It needs at least twelve weeks of history, however messy.

Spotto: the promise I would not make

Two months after launch I was asked to commit to the quarter's plan: 14 items by 30 June, with at most 10% left undone. I said I could not promise it. I found where work stalled: every handover for review or testing came back 6 times in 10. I defined "done" in written acceptance tests, tested releases myself, and planned one quarter at a time, naming what would be dropped when an urgent request came in. Work that came back fell from 61% to 21%. 13 of the 14 items were accepted eight days early. A forecast on that team's own record, run three years later, gave the plan an 86% chance of holding.

Questions people ask before they start

Is the free Scan enough on its own?

If you can still move the date or cut scope, yes. When neither can move, the Delivery Audit starts from the same calculation and finds what is holding the date back, like finished work that waits days for a review.

Do you need our estimates or story points?

No. The forecast uses what your team finished and when, not what anyone estimated.

How is this different from the dashboard we already have?

A dashboard shows how work moves through your team today. The forecast tells you where you end up, for a date you have already promised to someone.

How do I know the number is right?

It is not an opinion. It is your team's own last twelve weeks, played forward. The method and the data behind it are public, and running it again next month shows whether the date is getting closer.

We don't use Jira or Azure DevOps.

The free Scan connects to Jira Cloud and Azure DevOps. Working with another tracker like Linear? Tell me which one and we can find a solution.

Will you contact me afterwards?

No. No account, and nobody calls you. If you want to talk, you book it.
